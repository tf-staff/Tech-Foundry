PIG CCI DEVICE CONTROLLER - SUMMARY
Companion to project_handoff_notes.txt, which has the detail
Last updated: 2026-10-09


The device is a voice-coil-driven pig CCI (controlled cortical impact)
instrument. Current scope is one 19 mm tip at one impact velocity of 4 m/s,
across five impact depths - 14, 12, 10, 8 and 6 mm - with the two shallowest
at risk because they leave less travel in which to absorb the impact energy
without rebounding off the hard stop. Impact depth is set mechanically
rather than by servo control: the actuator runs all the way to a
full-extension hard stop at about 40 mm, because bringing 4 m/s to a halt
within roughly 10 mm of travel is not repeatable.

There are three deliverables. First, finish the testing GUI by adding a
pre-impact retract, a user-specified dwell of 100 ms to 1 s, and a
post-impact retract, including the motion strategy for each and a way to
record the whole sequence at high resolution, possibly using a rolling
buffer on the Galil. Second, build the operator-facing user GUI that
actually runs experiments. Third, connect the two, with the testing GUI
writing config files that the user GUI reads - and that integration should
be modular, since the testing GUI may be modified in parallel.

Architecturally, a Tkinter host application talks to a Galil DMC-31011 over
Ethernet, which commands a Copley amplifier in current mode driving the
voice coil, with an LVDT as the only position sensor. Nothing time-critical
runs on the host: arming generates a complete DMC program and downloads it
with its data arrays, and firing merely executes it and reads back the
result.

The chosen motion method is an open-loop feedforward current profile, which
has worked better than anything else tried. A jerk-limited quintic
trajectory is solved on the host in physical units, then sampled at two
different rates - position into contour increments embedded in the program
text, and acceleration into a feedforward voltage array downloaded
separately - and played back by two concurrent controller threads that sum
at the amplifier input. It was chosen because current produces force, so
computing current from the planned acceleration commands the right behaviour
from the outset, while the position loop stays in the circuit only to trim
the residual.

The main constraint on all of this is recording. The controller has a hard
3000-element array table, four recorded channels consume all of it, and at
the impact mode's measured 122 microsecond sample period that is about 91 ms
of capture - against a full sequence that will run to seconds once dwell and
both retracts are included. Recording is also non-circular and crops
silently when a move outruns it. Current is not measured anywhere, the
current trace being the commanded value scaled by a constant the operator
must keep in sync with the amplifier, and velocity is not recorded either
but differentiated from position on the host.

The LVDT has two stable magnetic states that differ by about 2.5 percent in
span, switched by coil current above a sharp threshold, and this was the
source of long-standing calibration drift. The plan is to pin the state
rather than compensate for it in software: a 10 amp pulse reliably sets
mode 2, every impact puts the sensor there anyway, so calibration is taken
in mode 2 and a plain two-point calibration is treated as sufficient.

The open questions are the pre-retract mechanism and whether it should
deliberately condition the LVDT, the hold strategy at full extension, the
post-retract strategy, and how to record the complete motion within the
controller's limits.
